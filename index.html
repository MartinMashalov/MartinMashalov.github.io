2026MSc thesis

Reinforcement learning on diffusion language models

Should post-training change a model’s weights, or the order it writes in? The answer turned out to be neither on its own.

Diffusion language models un-mask a sequence instead of writing left to right, so they face a choice autoregressive models never do: which position to reveal next. I ran a factorial over four models, two methods and two reward designs on HumanEval, MATH-500, MBPP and GSM8K. Re-fitting the ordering policy on the merged weights beat either method alone — and a checkpoint that had already been RL-tuned resisted everything, because its advantage signal had collapsed to nearly zero.

A go-to-market agent that finds people describing your problem on Reddit, qualifies them against your ICP, and drafts the outreach — with every send behind a human approval gate.

agent-fleet

Run thousands of concurrent LLM agent runs on a box that costs six dollars a month.

Agent work is almost entirely spent waiting on a model API, so the right shape is one asyncio supervisor with a bounded pool. The hard parts come after: an at-least-once queue with leases and idempotency keys, per-step checkpointing so a killed process resumes mid-run, and backpressure that never lets concurrency exceed its cap.

generative-eval

Generative models are usually judged by eye. This scores five of them against ground truth on two testbeds — and the tests disagree about which one is best.

First a mixture of Gaussians, where the true modes are known exactly — that arm exists to validate the instrument. Then seventeen years of real intraday equity bars, where the stylized facts of financial returns are a published answer key: fat tails, volatility clustering, the leverage effect. Four escalating tests, no human deciding whether a sample looks right.
